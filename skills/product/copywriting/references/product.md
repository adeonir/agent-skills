# Product register

When the words SERVE the task: app and dashboard UI, forms, settings, data tables, onboarding, states, and navigation. This applies whenever the reader is mid-task and the copy helps them through it.

## Posture

| | Product |
|---|---|
| Question | "Can the reader act without re-reading?" |
| Bar | Clarity: the right word, in plain language, at the right moment |
| Failure | Jargon, ambiguity, cleverness that slows the task |
| Permission | Brevity, consistency, instructional directness; personality saved for moments |

## The product slop test

Not "does it have a voice": clarity is the point here. The test: can a user fluent in the category act on this copy without pausing? Product copy's failure mode is not flatness; it is friction: jargon, vague labels, an error that never says how to fix it, or a button that does not name its action.

## Clarity and tone

- Be specific, active, human: "Save changes" over "OK"; tell the reader what to do, not only what happened.
- One term per concept, held across the surface (Delete, not Delete / Remove / Trash).
- Concise and calm; verbs first, no hype.
- Tone shifts with the moment (success, error, loading) on top of a constant voice (`voice.md`).

## Product bans (on top of the dead language in `anti-patterns.md`)

- Jargon or internal labels where plain user language belongs.
- Bare error codes or "Something went wrong" with no next step.
- Placeholder-only fields (the label vanishes the moment the reader types).
- Cleverness or humor in an error; the reader is already stuck.

## Register is not surface

**Surface** is the granular type the copy serves, and the register is the posture it sits under; see `discovery.md` for the definition. Surfaces under this register:

**Product surfaces:** dashboard, settings, form / wizard, data-table, onboarding, empty-state, navigation, checkout / account, authenticated tools. Storefronts straddle: the checkout / account microcopy is Product, the catalog copy is Brand (see `brand.md`).
