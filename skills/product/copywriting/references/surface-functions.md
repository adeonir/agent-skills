# Surface Functions

Classify `intent.function`, the reader's job, before selecting copy patterns or quality criteria.

## When to Use

Read before authoring or judging when the surface function is not explicit, when a surface has mixed jobs, or when conversion patterns may be mistaken for general copy rules.

## Choose the Function

- **conversion:** persuade the reader to choose, buy, sign up, contact, or otherwise commit. Use a value proposition, benefits, proof, objection handling, risk reduction, and a CTA when the surface needs a commitment.
- **brand/editorial:** express a point of view, build recognition, or hold attention. Use narrative, distinctiveness, rhythm, and memorable language. Add conversion patterns only when the surface also asks for a commitment.
- **product/UX:** guide a task, decision, state, or interaction. Use task-oriented headings, labels, buttons, states, navigation, and recovery guidance. Do not use conversion page shapes as a default.
- **informational:** explain a subject, process, policy, or result. Use hierarchy, definitions, examples, context, and caveats. Add a next step only when the reader needs one.

Never infer conversion from a page name alone. A homepage, product page, or landing page may be conversion, brand/editorial, informational, or mixed.

## Function-Specific Criteria

Use the function to set the quality bar:

| Function | Primary question | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion | Does the copy support a confident decision? | Vague value, weak proof, unanswered objections, or an unclear ask |
| Brand/editorial | Does the copy express a distinct point of view and earn attention? | Generic voice, borrowed language, or empty personality |
| Product/UX | Can the reader act without re-reading? | Ambiguous labels, missing next steps, or poor recovery guidance |
| Informational | Can the reader understand the subject and its limits? | Missing context, weak hierarchy, unsupported claims, or hidden caveats |

## Mixed Surfaces

Assign a function per surface when possible. For a mixed surface, assign the function per part and use only the patterns that serve that part. Preserve the shared voice and terminology across functions.
