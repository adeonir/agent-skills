# Brand register

When the words ARE the product: marketing sites, landing and campaign pages, launch announcements, portfolios, personal sites, about pages, and editorial surfaces. The copy's job is to make an impression and express a point of view; persuasion applies only when the intent supports a decision.

## Posture

| | Brand |
|---|---|
| Question | "Would a reader feel something, or skim past?" |
| Bar | Distinctiveness: a clear point of view, a voice that sounds like someone |
| Failure | Generic, on-brand-for-anyone, adjective soup |
| Permission | Bold claims (when proven), personality, rhythm, and a memorable line |

## The brand slop test

If the copy could sit on any competitor's page unchanged, it has no point of view. Brand copy earns attention with specificity and a voice: a concrete fact, a first-person observation, or a line only this author could write. For an informational intent, apply this test to the surface as a whole, not every factual line. Stacked adjectives ("innovative", "seamless", "world-class") are the tell; replace them with the specific thing. (See dead words and structures in `anti-patterns.md`.)

## Voice and proof

- Lead with the reader's outcome or problem when the surface supports a decision, never with company history; an informational surface leads with the subject.
- Support capability, quality, and outcome claims with proof. A factual description may stand on its context (proof hierarchy in `voice.md`).
- Commit to a voice and hold it; one distinctive line beats five hedged ones. Use an action-oriented CTA when the surface has an action to support.
- Personality is permission here: wit, rhythm, and a strong opener when the brand invites it.

## Brand bans (on top of the dead language in `anti-patterns.md`)

- Adjective stacks standing in for proof ("powerful, intuitive, seamless").
- Hedged claims that commit to nothing ("helps you do more").
- Borrowed-frame openers ("In today's fast-paced world…").
- A voice indistinguishable from the category's modal page.

## Register is not surface

**Surface** is the granular type the copy serves, and the register is the posture it sits under; see `discovery.md` for the definition. Surfaces under this register:

**Brand surfaces:** landing, campaign, launch announcement, portfolio, personal site, long-form / editorial, about, the marketing shell of a storefront. Storefronts straddle: the catalog copy is Brand, the checkout / account microcopy is Product (see `product.md`).
