# Rendered Evidence

Prepare evidence that lets critics judge the result they can observe.

## When to Use

Read before rendering a part and sending it to the critics.

## Capture

Use the rendering tools available in the session. Capture the result and reference at comparable sizes, states, and spans. When the criteria name a part of the reference, capture only that part. Verify each capture against the actual rendered view before using it.

- **Sites and applications:** Capture relevant viewports and interaction states. Walk through scroll and motion when they carry the reference's quality. Use viewport captures when a full-page capture misrepresents fixed elements, canvas, or video.
- **Videos and animations:** Capture enough consecutive frames to judge timing, movement, and continuity, not just a still image.
- **Documents and presentations:** Render pages or slides in sequence with their margins, figures, and layout intact.

Place the result and reference side by side for the visual critic. Use neutral A/B labels and keep the provenance map outside the critic's context. Give critics the rendered evidence, not implementation files or a description of the builder's intent. If the evidence cannot show a criterion, report that limit before requesting a verdict.
